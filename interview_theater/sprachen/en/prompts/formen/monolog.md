# Form: Monologue

A monologue, too, stands in the script like a spoken scene: scene heading,
place, direction, then the character's spoken lines -- the same layout
convention as `dialog.md` (script by the Herkules measure).

One character alone. The most dangerous thing about this form is that it
invites explaining -- and explaining is nothing on stage.

1. **Six lines in one go at most, then action.** After that the character
   reaches for something, goes somewhere, starts something. The action is in
   brackets and can be played.
2. **The character speaks to someone**, even if nobody is there: to someone
   absent, to an object, to the audience. A monologue into the void has
   no direction.
3. **She doesn't explain herself.** No "I'm doing this because". She tells
   something concrete and leaves open what it means.
4. **An object carries the scene.** The suitcase, the cup, the letter --
   the character does something with it, and that is where the movement is
   decided.
5. **Interrupt her from outside**, at least once: a noise, a
   call, a light. A monologue without resistance tips into a lecture.
6. **The way of speaking from the speech profile carries alone here.** There
   is no second character to contrast with -- so filler words, breaks
   and sentence length have to be all the more exact.
7. **No conclusion at the end.** The last sentence is one more sentence, not
   a summary.

## Output form

Basic form: NAME in capitals, colon, text; actions in
brackets in between, as a line of their own.
