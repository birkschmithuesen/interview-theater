# Style: Sharp back-and-forth with a hook

Template: "Schatten" -- Morpheuz x Monet192 (YouTube). What is taken over is
the build, never the content.

These rules only apply when you write scene text in a form (Dialogue,
Monologue, Chorus, Song, Rap) — not in the conversation and not in the prose version.
They come IN ADDITION to the Herkules measure and the form rules; where something
collides, the form file applies.

Measured on a German-language rap source (273 words): median 6.5
words per unit of meaning, 31 % word-for-word four-word repetition, 16× negation
in 273 words, I-to-you in almost every line.

**What is taken over is the build — never the content.** Motifs, images
and choice of words from the template are off limits.

## Rules

1. **Six to eight words per line.** Measured median of the template: 6.5;
   the longest unit had 19 words and was the exception. No sentence
   longer than two lines.
   > Evidence (verse 1, translated from the German): "Don't know if it was
   > love, / but something about us doesn't hold."
   > Own example: "Don't know if that was a room. / Two mattresses,
   > one door."
   > Not like this: "To be honest I still don't know exactly whether what
   > was between us could even be called that."

2. **Always address someone.** I talks to you. No line about someone, every
   line to someone. The monologue, too, is directed at an absent person.
   > Own example: "You took the key with you. / I stood
   > downstairs, half past six."
   > Not like this: "She took the key and left me standing downstairs."

3. **Question instead of statement.** In the template, questions pile up that
   nobody answers; that creates pressure without explanation.
   > Evidence (verse 2, translated from the German): "You were gone so long.
   > Say where you were."
   > Own example: "Where were you on Friday? / Say it, or say nothing at all."
   > Not like this: "I would really like to know where you were on Friday."

4. **Negation carries.** "Not", "no", "never" is the most frequent word of the
   template. Say what doesn't hold instead of what does.
   > Own example: "This isn't a fight. / This isn't a room. / This is
   > not my name."
   > Not like this: "It's something completely different from what it seems."

5. **One hook, word for word the same.** One or two lines come back unchanged
   — after the first and after the second verse. Don't change a word of it,
   vary nothing.
   > Evidence (hook, translated from the German): "You're only mine. / You
   > follow me like a shadow."
   > Own example (hook): "The bus goes at ten. / I'm already there."
   > Not like this: second time round as "The bus comes around ten, and I'm
   > waiting again" — varied, so no longer a hook.

6. **Rhyme impure and at the end of the line.** Two lines, an approximate
   sound match. A rhyme that bends the choice of words is cut.
   > Own example: "I count the stairs up to the top / and no one promised me
   > a lot."
   > Not like this: "I count the stairs from one to eight, / and all alone I
   > lie awake." — contrived, wrong register.

7. **Line break against the sentence structure.** The sentence runs over the
   edge of the line; the line ends where the breath ends, not where the full stop is.
   > Own example: "I said I'd come / later, if you want,
   > or not at all."
   > Not like this: every line a complete, neatly finished sentence.

8. **Concrete things, no concepts.** Time, place, object, number. No
   abstractions like trust, closeness, future.
   > Own example: "Two jackets on one hook. / One of them was mine."
   > Not like this: "Between us stood the missing trust."

9. **Self-reproach instead of accusation — at least once.** The template turns
   the anger inwards; that keeps the character sympathetic without excusing her.
   > Evidence (verse 2, translated from the German): "Asked myself why I'm
   > waiting."
   > Own example: "I ask myself why I'm still tidying up."
   > Not like this: "You're to blame for everything that happened here."

10. **Pauses are text.** The template leaves the music on its own 26 times.
    Put in empty lines and brief, playable direction instead of filler text.
    > Own example: "(Pause. She puts the bag down.)"
    > Not like this: "(Melancholy, with a hint of defiance in her voice.)"

11. **No conclusion at the end.** The template breaks off in the middle of a line.
    The last sentence is a twist or a remainder, not a moral.
    > Own example: "I closed the door. / From the inside."
    > Not like this: "And that's how I learned that you have to let go."

12. **Take nothing over from the template.** Forbidden are shadow and
    vicious-circle images, addressing someone as "baby", heartbreak as a theme and any
    line that sounds like the original. The content comes exclusively from
    the group's interviews.

## What this template is good for

- **Good:** Rap, Song, Dialogue with short lines, Monologue to an absent
  person.
- **With limits:** Chorus — the we-form contradicts rule 2; then only use
  rules 1, 5, 8, 10.
- **Not:** prose (phase 6) and calm, narrative passages. For a
  tender or reconciling scene this tone is too hard.
