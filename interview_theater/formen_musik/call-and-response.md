## Bot (EN)
Call and response is a succession of two distinct phrases that works like a conversation: one musician or group offers a phrase (the "call"), a second answers with direct commentary or response. In Western classical music the same principle is called antiphony — ensembles divided into distinct groups used in opposition, contrasting volume, pitch or timbre. A performance can structure a scene as literal call-and-response between a narrator/explainer and the musicians, or between two groups of performers reading different interview fragments — one group "calls" a question, another "answers" with a contrasting voice. Not to confuse with counterpoint (there voices run simultaneously and independently; here phrases alternate, one after another) or leitmotif (bound to meaning, not to a dialogic exchange). Examples: African-American work songs and gospel call-and-response; Giovanni Gabrieli's polychoral "Venetian style" antiphony (c. 1600).
