## Bot (EN)
Modulation is the change from one tonality (tonic and/or mode) to another within a piece — a shift of "home base" itself, not just a temporary departure from it. Modulations articulate or create the structure of many pieces: the most common type, common-chord modulation, moves smoothly via a chord shared by both the old and new key, acting as a hinge. Charles-Henri Blainville (1767) called it "the essential part of the art ... a piece derives its true beauty not from the large number of fixed modes which it embraces but rather from the subtle fabric of its modulation." A performance can use modulation as a structural marker for a change of perspective or location in the collage: shifting the whole ensemble's tonal "home" exactly when the narrative shifts to a new interviewee or a new place, so the audience feels the ground itself has moved, not just the topic. Not to confuse with tonicization (a brief, local reference to another key without actually changing "home") or with a cadence (a cadence confirms an existing key, modulation changes which key is "home").
