## Bot (EN)
A leitmotif is a short, recurring musical phrase tied to a specific person, place, object or idea; it stays identifiable even when transformed in rhythm, harmony, orchestration, or combined with other leitmotifs to signal a new dramatic situation. Unlike a plain refrain (rondo), a leitmotif is bound to MEANING, not to a fixed place in the form — it can surface anywhere the idea it represents becomes relevant. A performance can assign a short musical or vocal gesture to each interviewed person or theme; every time that person/theme reappears in the collage, the motif returns, subtly altered by context. Not to confuse with a rondo refrain (fixed formal position) or a simple repeated riff (no dramatic/semantic binding). Examples: Wagner, Der Ring des Nibelungen (1853–1869), hundreds of leitmotifs; Prokofiev, Peter and the Wolf (1936), one instrument/motif per character.
