## Bot (EN)
Variation is a formal technique where material is repeated in an altered form — melody, rhythm, harmony, timbre, or orchestration can all change, while the identity of the original theme stays recognizable underneath. A theme-and-variations piece states a theme (8–32 bars), then runs through a series of variations, each the same length and shape as the theme but transformed. This gives a performance a built-in way to revisit the same material from many angles: play an interview fragment once "straight", then let it return reshaped — slower, in a different voice, fragmented — each time revealing a new facet. Not to confuse with development (sonata form): variation presents one transformation at a time, development recombines fragments freely and simultaneously. Examples: Mozart, "Ah, vous dirai-je, Maman" (Twinkle Twinkle) 12 Variations (1785); Beethoven, Diabelli Variations (1819–23), 33 variations on a single waltz.
