A: What if the whole play centers on that image of waiting?
B: Right, the person who sits on the bench every single day, waiting for a cousin who never actually shows up.
C: And maybe the cafe across the street is where she gets her coffee, always the same table by the window.
D: She could carry that broken bag the whole time, like she refuses to buy a new one until he arrives.
A: The cafe woman might be the only one who really notices her, every single morning.
B: So the bench and the cafe become two fixed points, and the cousin is the absence that holds it all together.
C: I like that the bag stays broken the entire play, it is such a small, stubborn detail.
D: What if there is an old photograph in the bag, one she never takes out in front of anyone at the bench?
A: A photograph of the cousin as a child, maybe, taken right outside that same cafe before he left.
B: The cafe owner could remember him too, and that gives us a second person who carries the waiting.
C: I keep thinking about a train ticket folded inside the bag, a date on it that never comes again.
D: Could the bench itself have a small brass plate, a name carved into it that nobody explains yet?
A: Maybe the cousin wrote one letter, years ago, and the bag is really where that letter still lives.
B: The cafe window could reflect the bench, so the audience sees both the waiting and the watching at once.
C: I like a scene where the cafe owner finally sits down next to her on the bench, just once, without words.
D: What if the bag's broken strap is the exact reason she never managed to leave for the station in time?
A: A missed train could be the whole first half of the play, and the bench is where she replays it nightly.
B: The cousin might send a postcard instead of coming, and she keeps it zipped inside the broken bag.
C: Could the cafe play the same song every evening, and that song is what finally makes her stand up?
D: I want the bench to creak in a very particular way, so the audience hears her arriving before they see her.
A: Maybe the final image is the bag finally mended, sitting empty and open on the bench by the cafe door.
B: Or the cousin's chair at the cafe stays empty on purpose, and she starts leaving the bench for him.
C: Either way, the bench, the cafe, the cousin and the bag should stay locked together the whole play.
