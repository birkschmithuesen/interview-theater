## Bot (EN)
Theatre of the Absurd: term coined by critic Martin Esslin (1960/61) for post-WWII plays (1940s–60s) expressing meaninglessness of the human condition — chiefly Beckett, Ionesco, Adamov. Plot: static or circular, no purposeful development. Audience role: confronted with illogical stage images, not argued philosophy. Status of text: language devalued, dialogue often fails to communicate. Not to confuse with: existentialist drama (Sartre/Camus), which argues absurdity IN conventional form. Example: Samuel Beckett, "Waiting for Godot" (1952/53).
