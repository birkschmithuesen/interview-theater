# Form: Song

A song, too, stands in the script like a spoken scene: scene heading, place,
direction, then the lyrics as the spoken lines of the character(s) -- the same
layout convention as `dialog.md` (script by the Herkules measure).

A song is not a sung scene. It stops where the dialogue goes on:
a character (or several) says once what they would never say in a
conversation -- and may talk longer than usual for it.

1. **A song has one thought, not three.** What is in the verse
   is not continued in the chorus but drawn together.
2. **Concrete, not general.** "The suitcase has been on top of the wardrobe
   for thirty years" carries; "home is a feeling" doesn't. Take the
   objects, numbers and places from the details of the scene.
3. **The chorus repeats word for word.** Same lines, same number of
   syllables. At most the last word of the last repetition is varied,
   and only if something turns with it.
4. **Short lines, even.** Four to eight syllables. Whoever reads it aloud
   has to be able to breathe without rushing -- amateurs sing this.
5. **Rhyme is allowed, but never at any price.** An impure rhyme is
   better than a wrong word. No rhyme that bends the sentence.
6. **The character's way of speaking stays.** A song is not a place where
   suddenly everyone sings in textbook English: filler words, dialect and
   language mixing from the speech profile belong in it.
7. **No comment from outside.** The character sings the song, not a
   narrator about her. No "she thought back then".
8. **Three verses and two choruses at most.** Anything beyond that is a
   second song.
9. **At the end something is different.** A song, too, delivers the movement
   of the scene: the last chorus comes after something that wasn't true before.
10. **Stage directions stay brief and playable** -- who stands where, what is
    in their hand. No music instructions ("melancholy, in a minor key").

## Output form

Markers in capitals on a line of their own, then the lines of the
song. The markers stay exactly as written below (`STROPHE`, `REFRAIN`) --
they are read by machine. If a particular character sings, their name is in
the marker line:

```
(Living room, late evening. <CHARACTER A> is clearing the table.)

STROPHE (<CHARACTER A>)
The suitcase is still up there
for thirty years it's stood
...

REFRAIN
...

STROPHE (<CHARACTER A>)
...

REFRAIN
...
```

Spoken lines between the parts are allowed and then use the
basic form (NAME: line).
