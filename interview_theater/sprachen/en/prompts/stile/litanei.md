# Style: Litany -- one line, many times

Template: "Lovesong" -- Adele (YouTube). What is taken over is the build,
never the content.

These rules only apply when you write scene text in a form (Dialogue,
Monologue, Chorus, Song, Rap) — not in the conversation and not in the prose version.
They come IN ADDITION to the Herkules measure and the form rules; where something
collides, the form file applies.

Measured on a ballad (187 words, but only 32 different ones): 82 % of all
three-word sequences occur more than once, lines are the same length (approx. 7 words),
there is no build-up in content — only in frequency.

**What is taken over is the build — never the content.** Motifs, images
and choice of words from the template are off limits.

## Rules

1. **Build a sentence pattern with exactly one variable slot.** The sentence
   stays, one word changes. That is the main tool of this template.
   > Evidence (verse 1): "you make me feel like I am home again / … I am whole
   > again"
   > Own example: "When I'm standing here, I'm small again. / When I'm
   > standing here, I'm loud again."
   > Not like this: "Here I feel small, and sometimes quite loud as well."

2. **Repeat word for word, not in meaning.** No synonym, no elegant
   variation. What comes back comes back letter for letter.
   > Not like this: first "When I'm standing here", then "As soon as I'm here" — the
   > variation destroys the effect.

3. **Small vocabulary.** The template gets by with 32 different words.
   Choose twenty to forty words and stick with them; a new word
   is an event.
   > Own example: "Door. Stairs. Light. / Door. Stairs. No light."
   > Not like this: "Portal, stairwell, illumination" — variety for its
   > own sake.

4. **All lines the same length.** Six to eight syllables, one breath. The
   end of the line is the pause for breath; sentences don't run over the edge.
   > Own example: "I count to three. / I count again."
   > Not like this: "I count to three, but because nobody comes, I start
   > again from the beginning."

5. **The chorus comes back unchanged — at least three times.**
   > Evidence (chorus): "however far away / I will always love you"
   > Own example (chorus): "However far it is — / I'll go on foot."
   > Not like this: "heightening" or embellishing the chorus the second time.

6. **Build-up only through frequency.** No new argument, no new
   information. What is to get stronger is said more often.
   > Own example: "I'm waiting. / I'm waiting. / I'm waiting still."
   > Not like this: "I'm waiting — and slowly I realise it's pointless."

7. **No conflict in the text.** This template knows no opponent and no
   argument. The tension lies between the calm line and the situation
   around it.
   > Not like this: a line that accuses someone of something.

8. **Two voices share the pattern.** For Chorus and Dialogue: one
   speaks the fixed line, the other fills the variable slot. That way
   the litany stays without becoming monotonous.
   > Own example:
   > CHORUS: When I'm standing here, I'm back to being —
   > CHARACTER A: small.
   > CHORUS: When I'm standing here, I'm back to being —
   > CHARACTER B: loud.

9. **Concrete words in the variable slot.** What changes has to be
   tangible: state, place, object, age. No abstractions.
   > Own example: "… I'm twelve again / … I'm wet again / …
   > I'm here again."
   > Not like this: "… I'm authentic again / … I'm myself again."

10. **The ending is a minimal deviation.** One word more, one word
    less — no more than that. No new thought, no punchline.
    > Evidence (ending): "I'll always love you / cuz I love you"
    > Own example: "However far it is — / I'll go. On foot."
    > Not like this: "And in the end I know: the journey was the destination."

11. **Little direction, no mood instructions.** The text carries itself through
    repetition; stage directions stay brief and playable.
    > Not like this: "(tender, almost whispering, with tears in her eyes)"

12. **Take nothing over from the template.** Forbidden are
    "I will always love you" formulas, declarations of love as a theme,
    lines quoted from the song and any word-for-word borrowing. The content
    comes exclusively from the group's interviews.

## What this template is good for

- **Good:** Chorus (repetition is the chorus tool par excellence), Song, Monologue
  with the character of a litany.
- **With limits:** Rap — the flow can carry the pattern, but rule 6 (no
  build-up in content) contradicts the usual structure of a rap; then only use
  rules 1, 2, 5.
- **Not:** Dialogue with plot, argument scenes, prose (phase 6). Where something
  is negotiated or decided, the litany blocks the scene.

## Warning

This template quickly tips into droning. Rule of thumb: **twelve
lines in one go at most**, then an action, another voice or a break.
Amateur actresses have to be able to learn it by heart — the repetition helps
with that, the monotony doesn't.
