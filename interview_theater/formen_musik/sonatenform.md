## Bot (EN)
Sonata form structures a movement in three large parts: the exposition presents one or two contrasting themes in different keys (often framed as a "question"); the development explores, recombines and intensifies that material, often moving through distant keys; the recapitulation returns the themes to the home key, resolving the tension the exposition set up ("answering the question"). A performance can map this directly onto a dramaturgical arc: present two contrasting voices/themes (e.g. two interviewees with opposing views), let the middle section fracture and recombine their material, then let a final section bring both voices back "home" together. Not to confuse with theme-and-variations (there one theme is varied repeatedly, here two+ themes are juxtaposed and resolved) or with rondo (sonata form has no repeating refrain between episodes). Examples: any Haydn or Mozart first-movement symphony/sonata; Beethoven's expanded, dramatic use of the form (e.g. Symphony No. 3 "Eroica").
