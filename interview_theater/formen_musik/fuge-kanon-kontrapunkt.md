## Bot (EN)
Counterpoint is the art of combining independent melodic voices that remain distinct while fitting together harmonically. A canon is the strictest imitation: one voice states a melody, a second voice repeats it exactly at a time delay, overlapping the first (like a round). A fugue is the most developed contrapuntal form: a subject is stated alone, then imitated by each voice in turn (exposition), followed by episodes and further entries in related keys, closing with the subject's return in the tonic. A performance can let several speakers/musicians state the same text/motif in staggered entry — each voice independent, none subordinate — producing a dense, layered collage where no single voice dominates. Not to confuse with call-and-response (there one voice answers another in alternation; here all voices run simultaneously) or homophony (melody plus accompaniment — here all voices are equally important). Examples: J.S. Bach, The Well-Tempered Clavier (fugues); any round/folk canon ("Frère Jacques").
