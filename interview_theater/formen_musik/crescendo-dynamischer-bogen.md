## Bot (EN)
Dynamics are the variation in loudness between notes or phrases, notated with relative markings (p–pp–ppp "soft" to f–ff–fff "loud") rather than fixed decibel levels. A crescendo is a gradual increase in volume, a diminuendo/decrescendo its opposite — both operate over a span of time, not an instant, making them structural tools that can shape the architecture of a whole section by accumulating or releasing energy toward (or away from) its most important event. A performance can use a slow group crescendo to build toward the moment a key interview sentence is spoken — louder instruments, more voices joining in — then cut to sudden silence or a whispered diminuendo right after, making that moment land harder by contrast. Not to confuse with tension/resolution (there harmonic stability changes; here only volume changes — the two can run independently or together). Examples: Ravel's Boléro (one long, 15-minute crescendo); Beethoven's extended dynamic contrasts (sudden fp, sfz accents).
