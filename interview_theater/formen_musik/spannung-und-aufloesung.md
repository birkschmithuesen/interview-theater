## Bot (EN)
Consonance and dissonance are the basic poles of harmonic stability: a consonant chord sounds stable, at rest; a dissonant chord sounds unstable and "wants" to move to a consonance. This is how tonal music generates and releases expectation over time — tension accumulates, then resolves. A performance can build a scene around exactly this arc: instrumental dissonance rising under a reading, then resolving into a consonant chord at the moment a key sentence lands. Not to confuse with simple "loud vs. quiet" (that's dynamics, see crescendo) — tension/resolution is about chordal stability, independent of volume. Examples: any V7→I cadence resolving a dominant seventh's tritone; Wagner's Tristan chord, famously delayed in its resolution for nearly the whole opera.
