## Bot (EN)
A cadence is a harmonic arrival point — a progression of two or more chords, usually dominant to tonic (V→I), that creates a sense of resolution at the end of a phrase. The tonic is the "home" note/chord the music is always pulled back toward; an authentic cadence is the strongest way of arriving there, a half cadence stops short (ends on V), a deceptive cadence sets up the arrival and then swerves away (V→vi). In a performance, the group can agree on one chord as "home" and have the explainer announce every time the band is about to play it — or deliberately withhold it (deceptive cadence) to extend tension. Not to confuse with simply "the last note" — a cadence is a progression, not a single pitch. Examples: Beethoven, Piano Sonata No. 8, mvmt. III (perfect authentic cadence); Wagner, Tristan und Isolde, Prelude to Act I (famous deceptive cadence).
