# Form: Chorus

A chorus, too, stands in the script like a spoken scene: scene heading,
place, direction, then the spoken lines -- the same layout convention as
`dialog.md` (script by the Herkules measure).

Several people speak together. The chorus can do what no single character
can: say something that belongs to no one alone.

1. **We-form.** The chorus says "we", not "I". Where a single voice
   steps out, its name is there -- and afterwards it falls back into the we.
2. **Repetition is the main tool.** A line comes back, word for word
   the same. What changes is who says it and how often.
3. **Short lines.** What ten people are to speak at the same time has to be
   short and rhythmically the same. Long sentences fall apart in a chorus.
4. **Counting, listing, enumerating** carries: years, places, jobs,
   times of day. Take them from the details and the quotes, invent none.
5. **The chorus doesn't comment.** It claims, asks, lists -- but it
   doesn't explain what the play means.
6. **Single voices break the block open.** Every three to four chorus lines
   a character steps out with a sentence of their own; their way of speaking
   stays the one from their speech profile.
7. **At the end something is different** -- usually because a line that
   everyone said before is said by only one at the end (or the other way round).

## Output form

`CHORUS:` for the shared lines, the character's name for single voices.
Stage directions in brackets, brief:

```
(<place>, early evening.)

CHORUS: We've been waiting here for two hours.
<CHARACTER A>: I've got no signal.
CHORUS: We've been waiting here for two hours.
```

## Special case: chorus against chorus (battle)

Two groups speak in turn as a chorus (worked out 06.09.2026). The build is
borrowed from rap ("Schatten" -- Morpheuz x Monet192), never the content.

1. **A pair of lines is the unit:** A one line, B one below it. Never
   two lines in a row for the same group.
2. **Seven words per line at most.** Count them.
3. **The second line answers the first** -- picks up its word and
   turns it round. Two claims side by side are not a back-and-forth.
4. **Three to four pairs, then the hook:** ONE shared line, coming back
   word for word the same, at least twice.
5. **Insults follow the conflict, never at random** -- only the
   group's dividing lines. A diss without reference to the conflict is cut.
6. **Per chorus one line from a checked quote:** object, place, number --
   never the wording of the transcript, never a real name.
7. **A single voice breaks the block exactly once**, shortly before the end.
8. **No conclusion, no reconciliation** -- the ending is a twist.

```
INSIDERS: Whose is the courtyard? Our courtyard!
OUTSIDERS: Belongs to nobody. We're here anyway.
BOTH: Say what you like. We're staying.
```
